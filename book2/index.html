A birthday, read like a weather report

Old Master Qing Wu has spent forty years reading birth dates at a market table. In this book he teaches — to readers who did not grow up inside the tradition — how the Four Pillars work: the year, month, day, and hour each stamped with a pair of characters from a sixty-pair wheel, and what those eight characters say about the season you arrived in. Not what will happen. The season.

He starts from what you already know — your own tradition's ways of marking time — and then shows exactly where the Chinese machine differs, down to the two clocks every birth answers to: the clock on the wall and the sun's clock. Every quotation from the old manuals is printed in Chinese with a plain-English explanation, so you can check him line by line. Where the sources disagree, he shows the disagreement. Where he is guessing, he says so.

Available as a Kindle ebook from Amazon. The book carries the Chinese texts, the pinyin, three appendices — the sixty pairs at a glance, the terms of the trade, and full sources — and one worked example walked end to end, step by step, with every number checkable.

The calculator the book promises

The free companion tool: you write your question first, it records the date, it corrects the clock to the sun's time with every step shown, and it lays out the four pillars, the ten relations, and the three checks — and then it stops talking. No verdicts, no advice, and no record of your birth hour on any server: the arithmetic runs in your browser.

It names every table and rule it uses, it tests its own calendar arithmetic against two fixed anchor dates every time the page loads, and if a test ever failed it would switch itself off. A tool that can be corrected by its users is the only kind the book was willing to ship.

About Old Master Qing Wu

Old Master Qing Wu (青乌) spent forty years taking questions at a market table in a river town — hexagrams first, birth dates later, and refusals throughout. The Old Master Series is his table trade written down: Book One, The Old Master's Guide to the I Ching, and this volume, which teaches the calendar art the first book only pointed at.
